Why a Restaurant Guy Built an AI Roofer

Twenty years of turning tables taught me one thing that works in every business: tell people what happens next.

Home → Blog → Why a Restaurant Guy Built an AI Roofer

I spent twenty years in restaurants. Most of that time I was turning tables: getting guests seated, served, happy and out the door so the next party could sit down. It sounds like a numbers game. It isn't. The tables that turned smoothly were the ones where the guest always knew what was coming next.

From the dining room to the roof

When I partnered directly with The Exterior Company (TEC) to help homeowners after this year's storms, I ran into the same problem every contractor has. People don't call about something they don't understand. Most homeowners have never had an exterior inspection or filed a storm claim. They don't know what happens next, so they wait.

Enter Roofus

Roofus is an AI character I built on a laptop in Franklin. He isn't a real person, and every post says so. Every week he answers one question homeowners are actually asking: what does a free inspection check, what happens after damage is found, is my roof ready for snow.

Each episode becomes a short video, a blog post on the NW PA Roofing Quotes site, a Google Business update and a week of social posts. One idea turns into a week of useful content.

Why a character works when you don't want to be on camera

Plenty of contractors are great at the work and hate being filmed. A character carries the message every week without anyone standing in front of a camera. It stays consistent, it never has a bad day, and it gives your business a face customers start to recognize.

The restaurant rule behind every episode

Tell people what happens next, before they have to ask.

It takes the pressure out of almost any conversation. A guest who knows their entrée is ten minutes out relaxes. A homeowner who knows exactly what an inspection includes picks up the phone.

Could your trade have one?

HVAC, plumbing, landscaping, electrical, any home service where customers have questions they're a little afraid to ask. Your character answers one of them every week, and your phone number is at the end of every answer.
