Contractor Phones and Follow Up

Fall Is Busy Season for Your Phone. Here Is What Happens to the Calls You Miss.

Before the snow flies, Northwest PA homeowners call about roofs, furnaces, gutters and drafty windows. A simple plan keeps those calls from turning into someone else's jobs.

Home › Blog › Missed Calls Before Winter

By Bret Ruth, TableTurn Digital, Franklin, PA. Published September 25, 2026.

Every fall the same thing happens across Venango, Crawford, Warren, Forest, Clarion, Butler and Mercer counties. The leaves drop, the first cold night hits, and homeowners start thinking about everything they put off all summer.

The roof that dripped during the September storms. The furnace that has not been looked at in two years. The gutters packed with leaves. The back door that lets the wind in.

So they pick up the phone. And a lot of the time, the contractor they call is on a ladder, under a house, or driving between jobs.

That is not a failure. That is what a working contractor looks like. The real question is what happens to that call after you miss it.

What a Homeowner Usually Does After Your Phone Rings Out

I spent more than twenty years in hospitality and several more years making outbound calls all day. One thing both jobs taught me is that people rarely wait around when they have a problem. They move to the next option.

A homeowner with a leak or a cold house is not calling one company and sitting by the phone. They are working down a list. If your line goes to a full voicemail box, or a generic greeting that does not say who they reached, many of them simply hang up and call the next name.

You never see that job. It does not show up as a lost bid. It just quietly goes to whoever answered, or whoever called back first.

Why Fall Makes It Worse

In summer, a homeowner might be willing to wait a week to hear back about a deck or a patio. In fall the calendar is working against everyone.

  • Roof and siding work has a weather window, and homeowners know it is closing.
  • Heating calls feel urgent the first time the house drops below comfortable.
  • Your own schedule is fuller, so you are away from the phone more often.
  • Homeowners who waited all year now want an answer this week.

More calls, less time to answer them, and less patience on the other end. That is the combination that costs contractors good jobs between now and Thanksgiving.

Start With the Free Fixes

Before you spend a dollar, tighten up the basics. These cost nothing and take an afternoon.

  1. Record a real voicemail greeting. Say your company name, your first name, and when they can expect a callback. "You reached Example Roofing, this is Mike. I am probably on a roof right now. Leave your name, your town and what is going on, and I will call you back before the end of the day."
  2. Empty your voicemail box. A full box tells a homeowner nobody is paying attention.
  3. Pick two or three set times a day to return calls. Lunch and the drive home work for a lot of crews. Then keep that promise.
  4. Send a quick text when you cannot call right away. A short message buys you time and tells the homeowner a real person is on it.
"Hi, this is Mike with Example Roofing. Sorry I missed your call, I am finishing up a job in Oil City. I will call you by 5 today to talk about your roof. Reply here if a different time works better."

If you want more on what happens after that first reply, read why good contractor leads go cold. Most of the fixes are about follow up, not about getting more leads.

When the Free Fixes Are Not Enough

Some contractors are simply too busy to return every call the same day, especially in fall. If that is you, it is worth looking at having someone, or something, answer when you cannot.

There are a few ways to handle that:

  • A family member or office helper who answers during certain hours. Great if you have one.
  • A live answering service that takes messages for a monthly fee.
  • An AI phone agent that answers every call, tells the caller it is an automated assistant, collects their name, number, town and what they need, and passes that straight to you.

The AI option is newer, so here is the honest version. A well set up phone agent does not diagnose a roof, quote a price, promise a date or decide anything about insurance. Its job is to make sure the caller is greeted, their information is captured, and nobody hangs up on a ringing phone. You still make the callback and you still earn the job.

What it does change is that you stop finding out about calls days later, or not at all. You get the details in writing and you call back knowing who they are and what they need.

A Simple Fall Phone Checklist

  • My voicemail greeting says my company name and when I call back.
  • My voicemail box has room in it.
  • I have set times each day to return calls, and I keep them.
  • I send a short text when I cannot call right away.
  • Every missed call and form request gets written down in one place.
  • If I still cannot keep up, I have a plan for who or what answers.

Get those six things right and you will catch more of the fall work that is already calling you.
